# Daily strength routine

A machine-based push, pull, and legs routine, with light cardio (~90 min) at
the end of the day.

## Priorities

1. Joint safety comes before progression
2. Hard bike rides come before leg day
3. Build muscle and functional strength, more than cycling power

## Weekly schedule

The weekly hard ride is Thursday night and replaces that day's cardio.

| Day | Lifting | Cardio |
|-----|---------|--------|
| Monday | Legs | Easy |
| Tuesday | Push | Easy |
| Wednesday | Pull, kept comfortable | Very easy |
| Thursday | Rest | Hard ride (night) |
| Friday | Legs | Very easy |
| Saturday | Push | Easy |
| Sunday | Pull | Easy |

- Lift early, ideally at least 3 hours before cardio
- Monday is the latest leg day before the hard ride
- Wednesday: stay at least 2 reps in reserve (RIR), keep leg drive and heavy
  bracing out of rows, and go easier if back or grip is tired
- Don't make up missed sessions or move leg day closer to the ride
- Friday follows a night ride: skip legs if they aren't recovered

RIR is how many more good reps you could have done.

## Push

| Exercise | Sets × reps | RIR | Backup |
|----------|-------------|-----|--------|
| Chest press machine | 3 × 6–10 | 2 | Smith bench press |
| Incline press machine | 2 × 8–12 | 2 | Smith incline press |
| Shoulder press machine | 2 × 8–12 | 2 | Smith seated press |
| Lateral raise machine | 3 × 12–20 | 1 | Cable or dumbbell raise |
| Triceps extension machine | 2 × 10–15 | 1 | Overhead cable extension |
| Ab crunch machine | 3 × 10–15 | 1–2 | Cable crunch |

## Pull

| Exercise | Sets × reps | RIR | Backup |
|----------|-------------|-----|--------|
| Lat pulldown | 3 × 6–12 | 1–2 | Assisted pull-up, if getting on and off is comfortable and cleared |
| Seated row machine | 3 × 8–12 | 1–2 | Cable row |
| Reverse pec deck | 2 × 12–20 | 1 | Cable reverse fly |
| Biceps curl machine | 3 × 8–15 | 1 | Cable curl |

Use the Wednesday limits above when they're more conservative than this table.

## Legs

**Get these exercises, their ranges of motion, and loads reviewed by an
orthopedist or physical therapist.** Until then, only do ones your joints are
already known to tolerate. Review doesn't guarantee safety.

All are **only if cleared**:

| Exercise | Cautions | Backup (only if cleared) |
|----------|----------|--------------------------|
| Leg press | Pain-free range, stop short of locking knees, set safety stops if adjustable | None |
| Smith Romanian deadlift | Knees slightly bent, move mainly at hips, set safety stops | Dumbbell Romanian deadlift |
| Seated leg curl | Check alignment and pad position | Lying leg curl |
| Seated calf raise | Check pad position and comfort | None |
| Hip adductor machine | Ask about pad placement; pads at or below the knee can stress it sideways | None |

- Start with 1 set per exercise, building to no more than 2
- Use light loads for ~12–20 controlled reps, at least 3 RIR
- Don't force the top of the rep range, and never test a max
- Change only one thing at a time (weight, reps, sets, or range), after
  several well-tolerated sessions
- Leg extensions, hack squats, Smith squats, split squats, and calf raises on
  the leg press or Smith machine are left out. Add one only if cleared, and
  consider its knee demands first
- Aim for at least one good leg session most weeks. If rides often prevent
  this, change the schedule

## Stop and check

- **Stop the exercise** for sharp or increasing pain, painful catching or
  grinding, or a joint that feels like it may give way
- **Next day:** with new swelling, more pain or stiffness, or a limp, don't
  progress. Skip leg lifting until it settles, and get checked if it persists
  or returns
- **Get checked promptly** if a knee locks, gives way, can't bear weight, or
  becomes very swollen
- **Seek urgent care** if a very swollen knee is also hot or red, or comes
  with a fever
- **Recovery:** if performance drops, soreness lingers, sleep worsens, joints
  nag, or legs feel heavy on the bike, cut sets by about half or rest. Joint
  symptoms also follow the rules above

## Hard rides on other days

Rides replace that day's cardio. When in doubt, favor the ride.

- **Before a planned hard ride:** reduce or skip leg day if it could affect the
  ride, and don't push upper body close to the limit the day before a
  demanding mountain bike ride
- **After a hard ride:** skip leg day if legs aren't recovered or it could
  affect the next hard ride. If legs feel good, do up to about half the sets
- **Mountain biking:** on a moderate or hard ride day, skip pull. On an easy
  ride day, ride first, then pull, or skip it if tired
- **If the weekly hard ride suffers:** reduce Monday leg sets, or skip them.
  Also consider sleep, food, heat, and other riding

## How to progress

- For the first two weeks, do one less set per upper body exercise and stay
  further from failure. Extend this after a long break
- Take a few warm-up sets
- Rest ~2–3 min after presses, rows, and leg compounds, and ~1–2 min after
  other sets
- Upper body: when every set reaches the top of its rep range at the target
  effort with consistent form, add a little weight. If the jump is too big,
  add reps first
- Legs: follow the more cautious rules above
- Track strength as well as appearance
- Eat enough overall, including protein, and fuel hard rides with
  carbohydrates before, during as needed, and after

## Approximate upper body volume

Full planned sets per week. A set that works a muscle indirectly counts as
half.

| Muscles | Sets per week |
|---------|---------------|
| Back, biceps | ~12 |
| Triceps | ~11 |
| Chest | ~10 |
| Side delts | ~8 |
| Rear delts | ~7 |
| Abs | ~6 |

Optional, once well recovered: add 1 set of lateral raises to each push day.

## Optional functional work

Add only after the main routine feels manageable.

| Exercise | Amount | Knee demands |
|----------|--------|--------------|
| Pallof press, end of one push day | 2 sets per side, starting light | Standing brace against a sideways pull, so check that it's cleared |

Farmer carries are on hold until cleared, since they load the knees while
walking.

## Research

These findings come from study abstracts. The abstracts don't show that this
combination of daily lifting and ~90 min of cardio was tested. The exercises,
schedule, set and effort targets, and safety adjustments are practical
recommendations, not findings from these studies.

- More weekly sets generally meant more muscle growth, with diminishing
  returns. The effect of frequency on growth was compatible with negligible
  ([Pelland 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w))
- Muscle growth improved as sets ended closer to failure, but the exact
  relationship is unclear
  ([Robinson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2))
- Aerobic training didn't compromise whole-muscle growth or maximal strength
  overall, but could reduce explosive strength gains, especially in the same
  session ([Schumann 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-021-01587-7))
- Muscle fiber growth may be slightly reduced with added aerobic training
  ([Lundberg 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01688-x))
- With equal volume, split and full-body routines had little difference in
  effect on strength and muscle growth in 4–12 week programs
  ([Ramos-Campo 2024](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000004774))
